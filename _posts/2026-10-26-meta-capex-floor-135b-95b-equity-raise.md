Two capex signals pointed in opposite directions last week. Meta raised its spending floor and said it would tap equity markets to fund the buildout; Tesla cut. Per Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance, investors punished both, and the next test — Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon earnings plus a Fed decision — arrives this week.

## What Happened

| Company | Date | Development | Market reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta | Oct 21 | 2026 capex floor lifted to $135B; Q3 FCF reported at ~$784M (TradingKey) and described as turning negative (Seeking Alpha); one analysis implies Q4 capex of $49-59B | Fell ~3% after hours/next day; reportedly down ~9% over the week (TIKR) |
| Meta | Oct 21-22 | Plan to raise up to $95B via stock offering to fund AI; new non-voting share class | Buyback halted in 2026 now replaced by issuance (Yahoo Finance) |
| Tesla | Oct 22 | 2026 spending plan trimmed by about $5B | Global risk-off day alongside Middle East tension (Fortune) |

Meta's quarter also included a $9.9B legal charge that hit GAAP earnings (investingLive), so the EPS miss reported by some outlets is partly non-recurring; the capex and funding signals are the lasting part.

## Why It Matters

Goldman's Sept 25 forecast of $1.2T combined hyperscaler capex in 2027 (see our [Sept 27 post]({{ '/sp500/2026/09/27/goldman-ai-capex-1-2-trillion-2027/' | relative_url }})) flagged that capex already exceeds operating cash flow and would increasingly be debt-financed. Meta's move is the first concrete case of the gap being closed with **equity** — a $95B raise is large relative to the capex it funds, and it dilutes existing holders. That is a new data point for the "funding strain" side of the thread, not a sign of slowing demand: the floor went up, not down.

Tesla's cut is a much smaller number and a different business (its AI/robotics spend is a fraction of Big Tech's roughly $750B), so read-through to the hyperscalers is limited — though it fed the "end of the AI trade" narrative on Oct 22.

## Investment Read

- Supply chain (chips, power, memory) benefits directly from a higher Meta floor; watch Vertiv (Oct 28) and other suppliers for confirmation.
- The key question this week is whether Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon also raise guidance and whether funding shifts further toward debt/equity. A pattern of "raise capex + raise capital" would raise dilution and credit-risk questions even as it confirms demand.
- Caveat: several figures above come from secondary outlets and headline summaries; verify against Meta's investor-relations release before acting.

## 왜 중요한가

9월 25일 골드만의 2027년 하이퍼스케일러 합산 capex $1.2조 전망([9월 27일 글]({{ '/sp500/2026/09/27/goldman-ai-capex-1-2-trillion-2027/' | relative_url }}))은 capex가 이미 영업현금흐름을 넘어 점점 부채로 충당될 것이라고 짚었다. 메타의 이번 행보는 그 격차를 **주식 발행**으로 메우는 첫 구체적 사례다. $950억은 조달 대상 capex에 비해 매우 큰 규모이며 기존 주주 지분을 희석시킨다. 수요 둔화 신호가 아니라(하단은 오히려 올랐다) '자금 부담' 쪽의 새 데이터다.

테슬라의 삭감은 금액이 훨씬 작고(빅테크 합산 약 $7,500억 대비) 사업도 달라 하이퍼스케일러로의 시사점은 제한적이다. 다만 10월 22일 'AI 트레이드의 끝' 논쟁에 불을 붙였다.

## 투자 관점

- 메타의 하단 상향은 반도체·전력·메모리 공급망에 직접적 호재다. 10/28 버티브(Vertiv) 등 공급사 실적에서 확인할 필요가 있다.
- 이번 주 핵심은 마이크로소프트·알파벳·아마존도 가이던스를 올리는지, 자금조달이 부채·주식으로 더 이동하는지다. 'capex 증액 + 자본조달' 패턴이 확산되면 수요는 확인되더라도 희석과 신용 리스크 논란이 커질 수 있다.
